# Script Voice and Style

Patterns extracted from [past videos](past-videos-catalog.md). Use these to maintain consistency when co-drafting scripts.

## Voice

- **Conversational and direct.** Addresses the viewer as "you." Uses contractions freely. Reads like a knowledgeable friend explaining something, not a textbook.
- **First person singular.** "I've been using…", "Let me show you…", "I decided to take a look."
- **Confident but honest.** States opinions clearly, acknowledges limitations and tradeoffs without hedging excessively. Will say "it's not without drawbacks" and then list them plainly.

## Humor Style

- **Pop culture analogies** — Matrix/Neo (agent skills), Detective Pikachu / Sherlock Holmes (observability), The Good The Bad and The Ugly (data periods). These are quick references, not extended metaphors.
- **Self-deprecating jokes** — "which to be fair, won't be uncommon" (about making mistakes), "JP stares blankly into the void for an hour" (bad video title).
- **Absurdist escalation** — "It's like if Neo's new skill hadn't improved his fighting, but given him balance problems or caused him to directly punch himself repeatedly in the face."
- **Running gags within a video** — e.g., the Neo/skill metaphor returns multiple times in the agent skills video; Phantom Menace → Morbius bit.
- **Dry understatement** — "That's arguably worse" (about giving agents Docker socket access).
- **Deliberately cheesy moments** — acknowledged as cheesy: "wink and put up deliberately cheesy graphic."

## Tone Shifts

- **Hook:** Punchy, slightly provocative. Often poses a question or relatable frustration.
- **Explanation sections:** Measured, clear, authoritative. Technical accuracy matters.
- **Demo/screencast:** More casual, reactive. Bullet points rather than full scripting.
- **CTA/outro:** Warm, direct. Asks for engagement genuinely ("I read every comment"). Sometimes humorous ("tell your friends and pets").

## Phonetic Guidance

- Scripts include pronunciation guides inline for numbers/terms: "two thousand and fourty-eight (2048)", "sixty-eight hundred sessions".
- This suggests the scripts are written to be read aloud, and ambiguous numbers should always have pronunciation noted.

## Sponsor Integration

- Product mentions are woven into the narrative, not bolted on. Elasticsearch appears as part of the demo/solution, not as a standalone ad read.
- Occasional tongue-in-cheek self-awareness: "I should shamelessly plug…"

## Things to Avoid (inferred)

- Jargon without explanation — technical terms always get a popup or inline definition.
- Dry, monotone sections — even benchmark walkthroughs get framed as a story ("the picture is consistent").
- Overpromising — limitations and tradeoffs always get their own section.
