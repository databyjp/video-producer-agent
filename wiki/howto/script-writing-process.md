# Script Writing Process

Step-by-step workflow from blank page to speakable draft. For the underlying principles behind each step, see [Writing for the Ear](../writing-for-the-ear.md).

The through-line: **the script is a draft until it's been spoken**.

---

## Phase 1 — Structure only

Write the argument or narrative without touching language or voice. What does the viewer need to understand, and in what order? Lock this before anything else.

No jokes, analogies, or personality. Those come later.

### Define the viewer job

Write one sentence describing the primary job the viewer hired the video to do:

- **Learn** — understand one idea or mechanism.
- **Build** — complete one practical outcome.
- **Choose** — make a decision between options.
- **Diagnose** — recognize or solve one problem.

One job must dominate. If the outline gives two or more jobs equal weight, narrow the scope or split the concept into separate videos.

### Audit the packaging promise

Write the title-and-thumbnail promise in one sentence, then label the first point where the script delivers tangible evidence of that promise—a result, demonstration, answer, or decision. Move that evidence earlier if the opening spends too long on context.

Check every major section against the promise. Cut material that is interesting but does not advance it. When using an external story, transition into JP's own experiment or application before the story becomes the subject of the video.

*Source: LWT model — the joke-free outline forces you to solve the structural problem and the voice problem separately, rather than both at once.*

---

## Phase 2 — Speak a first draft

Don't type the first draft cold. Speak it.

Option A: dictate into a transcription app and edit the transcript.
Option B: write as if speaking — contractions, short sentences, informal vocabulary — and immediately read each paragraph aloud as you finish it.

The spoken draft skips the "revert to written register" problem because it starts from speech, not from prose.

*Source: journalism.university dictation method.*

---

## Phase 3 — Perform and revise

Read the full draft aloud and record yourself. Then watch or listen back.

Rewrite toward what you actually said, not what you intended to say. Every phrase where you instinctively rephrased, compressed, or stumbled is the script telling you something. That's the revision.

This is the most important step most people skip. It is a **writing** step, not a practice step.

*Source: Daily Show bunker rewrite — after rehearsal, the team projects the script on a wall and rewrites based on what the performance revealed. The script isn't final until it's been spoken to something that can respond.*

---

## Phase 4 — Mechanics pass

With a draft that sounds right, do one focused pass for mechanics:

- Spell out every numeral (`three hundred`, not `300`)
- Add line breaks, `[PAUSE]`, emphasis marks at delivery moments
- Find any technical sentence with an embedded clause — cut the clause free, make it its own sentence

See [Writing for the Ear](../writing-for-the-ear.md) for the full rules reference.

---

## Phase 5 — Pre-record ritual

Immediately before recording:

1. **Silent read** — content and comprehension; flag anything still stiff
2. **Aloud read** — rewrite anything that trips you
3. **On the app at recording speed** — calibrate scroll, column width, eye-lead
4. **Two warm-up passes** before the final take

If a phrase trips you three times: rewrite it. Familiarity is not the fix.
