# YouTube Help — Search, Discovery, and Content Performance

This summary consolidates current official YouTube Help guidance. It is the authoritative source for claims about YouTube platform behavior; it does not provide deterministic ranking formulas or universal performance thresholds.

## Recommendations

YouTube finds videos for individual viewers rather than "promoting a video to an audience." Recommendations use a viewer's watch and search history, how similar viewers responded, and how the video performs when recommended.

Official performance lenses:

- **Appeal:** whether viewers choose to watch, ignore, or mark a video "not interested."
- **Engagement:** whether viewers stick around, including average view duration and average percentage viewed.
- **Satisfaction:** whether viewers enjoy the experience, informed by signals such as likes and post-watch surveys.

YouTube uses both absolute and relative watch time. Relative watch time is generally more important for shorter videos; absolute watch time is generally more important for longer videos. The appropriate length is the length that satisfies the viewer's intent.

## Search

YouTube Search considers:

1. **Relevance** — how well title, description, tags, and video content match the query.
2. **Engagement** — how viewers interact with videos for that query.
3. **Quality** — signals of expertise, authoritativeness, and trustworthiness on a topic.

Tags play a minimal discovery role and are mainly useful for common misspellings.

## Packaging and experiments

Titles and thumbnails should accurately represent the video. A high click-through rate paired with low watch time is a clickbait pattern that can lead to fewer recommendations.

Eligible creators with Advanced features can use YouTube Studio A/B testing to test up to three titles, thumbnails, or combinations. The winner is selected by watch time, not CTR alone.

## Metadata and publishing

- Video descriptions should be unique and begin with a concise, useful summary; YouTube supports default-description boilerplate.
- Chapters are optional navigation aids. Manual chapters start at `00:00`, require at least three entries, and each entry must be at least ten seconds.
- Publish time is not known to affect long-term performance, though publishing when the audience is active can help initial viewership.
