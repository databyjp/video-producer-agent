# Clerk YouTube Creator Program

HyperGrowth Partners playbook. Authors: Alex Rapp, Gonto, Nick Parsons, Matteo Tittarelli.

## Key Points

### What Failed: Dedicated Brand Videos
- Initial approach: pay creators to make videos solely featuring Clerk.
- Result: **awareness with negative sentiment** — "These guys are paying people to talk good about them."
- No meaningful impact on sign-ups or activations.

### What Worked: Organic Integration + Full Creative Freedom
- New approach: creators use Clerk as *one tool in the stack* for content they chose (e.g. "How to build a ChatGPT clone").
- Creators got full creative authority, no deadlines, permission to flag product bugs.
- **30% increase in YouTube-attributed sign-ups** and strongest installation rate of any channel.
- Grew from 3–5 creators to 20 active partners.

### The Dark Funnel / Halo Effect
- On days partner videos went live, sign-ups attributed to *direct traffic and organic search* spiked — not just tracked links.
- Developers actively avoid being tracked. Much of the value is in unmeasured brand lift.
- Similar to IBM Technology's finding: you can't fully attribute the value, but it's real.

### Creator Autonomy Is Non-Negotiable
- "At the end of the day, we learned that developers don't want to just learn new frameworks and services in a vacuum, but in the context of learning how to create entire apps."
- Creative control attempts backfire. The authenticity that makes content trustworthy is destroyed by approval processes.

### Full-Funnel Impact
- Not all partners equally good at conversions — some drive top-of-funnel awareness, others drive sign-ups.
- Both are valuable. Brand awareness partners fill the top of the funnel.

## Relevance to Production Guidelines
- Validates the "concept first, product second" principle with hard data.
- Product-forward content creates negative sentiment. Organic integration works.
- The business case for viewer-first content is empirically proven.
