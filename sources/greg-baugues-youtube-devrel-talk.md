# YouTube Based DevRel — Greg Baugues

Talk at DevRelCon New York 2025. Greg Baugues, ex-Twilio DevRel (9 years), on growing a personal YouTube channel from 0 to ~8,500 subscribers.

## Key Points

### Why YouTube for DevRel Now
- The old playbook (events + blog posts) is weakening: developers increasingly solve problems via AI in the IDE rather than searching blogs.
- YouTube has superior measurement — you know exactly who's watching, for how long, and where they drop off.
- Developer YouTubers have an unfair advantage: you only need to be pretty good at coding + pretty good at speaking + competent at video. That combination is rare.

### Packaging > Content (Patty Galloway Principle)
- "YouTube is a click and watch platform. You can make the best video in the world, but if nobody clicks on it, no one's going to see it."
- Galloway recommends spending ~30% of effort on concepts, titles, and thumbnails *before any recording*.
- Baugues's first 10K+ video ("Cursor vs Claude Code") came from studying MrBeast's "X vs Y" format and adapting it to dev tools. That video hit 75K views.

### Respecting Viewer Time
- On his Claude Code vs OpenAI Codex video, Baugues broke YouTube best practices by spoiling the conclusion upfront. Comments overwhelmingly thanked him: **"thank you for respecting my time."**
- His "47 Claude Code Tips in 9 Minutes" video: ~50–60 hours of work compressed into 9 minutes. Now has 115K views and 6,000+ hours of total watch time. "I'm taking sixty hours of my time and compressing that down as a gift."
- Core philosophy: **invest your time to save the viewer's time at scale**.

### Production Process
- Video production has all the estimation problems of software + writing + editing combined.
- Film like you edit: modular chunks. Invest most editing effort in the first minute.
- The back half of a video can be less polished once the viewer is invested.

### Consistency and Psychology
- Publishing schedule matters: goal of 1 video/week (actually ships every ~10 days).
- "The internet doesn't care if you ship today, or tomorrow, or ever" — you have to create your own deadlines.
- Loneliness is the hardest part of solo YouTube. Support networks (group chats, peers) are essential.
- Delete bad comments without guilt. "I almost never get bad comments from anyone who has shipped a video."

### The Casey Neistat Lesson
- Want to work at a company? Make content about their product instead of submitting a resume. Baugues made a Claude Code tutorial (115K views), and Anthropic reached out.

## Relevance to Production Guidelines
- Packaging (title + thumbnail) is not an afterthought — it's a prerequisite.
- "X vs Y" format validated independently of Keen/IBM.
- Respecting viewer time is a developer-specific value that overrides generic YouTube engagement tactics.
- First minute gets disproportionate editing investment.
