# AIR Media Tech — YouTube Title Study (18,000 Channels, 11 Niches)

Analysis of titles across 18,080 English-language YouTube channels (100K–100M monthly views, Shorts-heavy channels excluded) measured against engagement rates and p75 views. **Correlational, not causal** — title patterns are associated with performance but don't prove the title alone caused it.

## Universal Findings

### Length

- **30–50 characters is the universal sweet spot.** Outperforms every other bucket across almost every niche and tier.
- Under 30 wins in Gaming, Entertainment, and Food & Drink only.
- **90+ characters consistently underperform** on views — truncation kills them on mobile (YouTube truncates at ~60–70 chars). Exception: search-optimized content where query match matters more than browse CTR.
- Counterintuitively, 90+ char titles sometimes show higher engagement *among those who click* — reflecting search-driven audiences.

### Numbers

- Numbers help in **Tech, Business, Fitness, and Food**; do little elsewhere.
- Work when they make a specific promise ("30-day challenge," "$50K in 6 months," "3-ingredient pasta").
- Don't work when decorative ("7 gaming tips").
- Business/Finance shows the clearest positive number effect (dollar figures, percentages, timeframes).

### Questions

- Work when the viewer already has the question — strong in **Education, Business, Science & Tech**.
- Fail in Gaming, Food & Drink, Kids — niches where viewers aren't in "seeking mode."
- Clickbait risk: YouTube suppresses questions that don't get answered in the video.

### ALL CAPS

- **Most niche-dependent pattern.** Strong positive in Gaming/Entertainment. Actively negative in Education.
- Rule: match the energy level of your niche. High-energy signals in low-energy niches create a mismatch.

### Brackets and parentheses

- Modest consistent positive across most niches.
- Function as format signals: [2026], (Full Tutorial), [Data Study].
- **Year-bracketing strongest in Tech, Business, Education** — audiences avoiding outdated info.
- Weakest in Gaming/Entertainment where bare titles outperform.

### Emotional words

- **Curiosity** ("secret," "hidden," "what nobody tells you," "the real reason") — the only positive that transfers cleanly across all niches and channel sizes.
- **Urgency** — positive at smaller sizes (100K–1M), neutral/ignored at scale.
- **Positive sentiment** ("best") — works for ranking/comparison intent, not as vague superlative.
- **Negative sentiment** ("worst," "failed") — works for failure stories and mistake-avoidance, not manufactured outrage.

### Keyword position

- Front-loading keyword in first 40% of title improves search ranking and survives truncation.
- For browse/discovery traffic, **hook strength outweighs keyword placement**.
- Practical: for search-driven content, keyword first. For browse discovery, hook first and let keyword land naturally.

## Niche-Specific: Science & Tech (Most Relevant to DevRel)

- Audiences are intent-driven, arrive with a question, click the title that most convincingly promises a credible answer.
- **[Year] bracket** is most pronounced in tech — viewers actively avoid outdated advice.
- 30–50 characters with clear subject + year signal is the default.
- Short titles (under 30) only work for established creators with strong brand recognition.

## Case Studies

- Title + thumbnail cleanup: CTR 5.6% → 6.4%, engaged views **+175%**, watch time **+201%**. Mechanism: removing clickbait-ish signals let the algorithm treat the channel as credible.
- Title rewrites across 3 channels: one gained +50% views/+97% revenue. Mechanism: better-matched audience = higher CPM.
- Metadata translation to 9 languages: +148% views, +97% subscribers. Recommended traffic exceeded 45% of views.

## Key Insight

> A title never works alone. Every click decision happens on a **thumbnail-plus-title unit**. Optimizing one half without the other leaves potential unrealized.
